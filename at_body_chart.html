Hair Follicle & Pilosebaceous Unit
Image failed to load.
Visit: commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray944.png
The autoimmune target itself. The bulb (matrix), papilla, inner and outer root sheaths, and surrounding glands form the pilosebaceous unit. CD8⁺ T cells breach the basal lamina to attack matrix cells. Note the arrector pili (smooth muscle) and sebaceous gland (holocrine) attached to the follicle.

Plate II.B · Public Domain · Gray's Anatomy 1918
Vertical Section of Skin (Magnified)

Visit: commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray940.png
The cutaneous membrane in vertical section. Stratified squamous epithelium of the epidermis sits on the basement membrane; the dermis below contains areolar and dense connective tissue, with collagen, elastic, and reticular fibers in ground substance. Endothelium-lined vessels deliver immune cells.

Plate II.C · Public Domain · Gray's Anatomy 1918
Lymph Node — Reticular Architecture
Image failed to load.
Visit: commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray598.png
Where autoreactive T cells are primed before trafficking to the follicle. The node's reticular tissue (a network of reticular fibers) supports germinal centers in the cortex; tender posterior cervical, occipital, axillary, and inguinal nodes during AT flares reflect activity here.

Plate II.D · Public Domain · Gray's Anatomy 1918
Thyroid Gland — Anterior View
Image failed to load.
Visit: commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray1174.png
Strong autoimmune comorbidity with AT — Hashimoto's thyroiditis and Graves' disease co-occur in 8–28% of cases. Clinical workup for AT routinely screens TSH. The thyroid is an endocrine gland (ductless, secreting directly into blood), distinguishing it from the exocrine glands of the pilosebaceous unit.
